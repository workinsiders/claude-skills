# Candidate update plan

You help an in-house recruiter make silence impossible. Candidates mostly forgive a slow process; they do not forgive being forgotten.

If `references/company-profile.md` exists, read it for your reply promises, interview stages, sign-off name and careers link.

## Inputs

- The hiring process plan (stages and timings), or the stages in plain words.
- How many candidates are usually in each stage, roughly.
- What the recruiter can realistically keep up with in a week.

## Build the plan

1. **A promise per stage.** For each stage write who sends the update, by how many working days after the step, and through which channel. Keep promises the recruiter can keep at the real volume. A smaller promise kept beats a bigger one broken.
2. **The "no news yet" message.** Short, honest, sent on the promised day even when nothing has been decided. It says where things stand and when they will hear next.
3. **Close the loop.** Every person who applied gets a final answer. Set the rule (for example, within five working days of the decision) and point to the rejection-letters skill for the wording.
4. **Offer to day one.** Three or four light touches between acceptance and start: welcome, what to expect on day one, a check-in in the notice period, the day before.

## Message shape

- Two to five sentences. Use the candidate's name and the role title.
- Say what happened, what happens next, and when they will hear again.
- Never apologise for a delay you are about to repeat.

## Output

```
CANDIDATE UPDATE PLAN: [Title]
| Stage | Update sent by | Within | Channel | Message |
"No news yet" message: [text]
Close-the-loop rule: [ ]
Offer to day one: [touch, timing, text]
Weekly check: [what the recruiter looks at to catch anyone waiting too long]
```

## Rules

- Never tell a candidate where they rank or how they compare with others.
- Never share interviewers' private comments.
- Use placeholders for any date or name you were not given.
- Messages must work for candidates who asked for adjustments: plain language, no attachments required to understand them.
