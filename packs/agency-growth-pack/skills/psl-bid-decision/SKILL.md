# PSL bid decision

A preferred supplier list can bring steady job orders, or capped fees, slow payment and no roles at all. You help an agency decide with its eyes open, and plan a strong response when it is worth it.

If `references/agency-profile.md` exists, read it for specialties, markets, fees and proof points.

## Input

The RFP, PSL invitation or panel terms, plus anything the recruiter knows: why it is being run, the incumbent agencies, past roles at this client.

## Step 1: the gates

Answer each from the document or the recruiter; unknown stays unknown.

1. **Is it real?** Is there a budget and a hiring plan, or is it a price check? Who runs it: the client, an MSP, procurement?
2. **Can we win?** Do our specialties match the roles? Do we know anyone there? Who are we up against?
3. **Is it worth it?** Fee caps or rate cards against our terms, payment terms, VMS fees, volume expected, number of suppliers on the panel, any exclusivity.
4. **Can we deliver?** Coverage, compliance requirements (insurance, background checks, diversity reporting, SLAs).
5. **What will it cost to write?** Hours, and who.

## Step 2: decision

Go, no-go, or go with conditions (questions to ask the buyer first). Give the reasons in three lines.

## Step 3: if go, the response plan

- Every requirement in the document, listed, with the answer owner and the proof to use.
- Win themes: two or three, each backed by a placement or case study.
- Questions to submit by the clarification deadline.
- Timeline back from the due date.

## Output

```
PSL / RFP: [client] | due [date]
Gates: real [ ] | can win [ ] | worth it [ ] | can deliver [ ] | cost to write [hours]
Decision: go / no-go / go if [ ]
Reasons: [3 lines]
Requirements map: | Requirement | Our answer | Proof | Owner |
Win themes: [ ]
Clarifying questions: [ ]
Timeline: [ ]
```

## Rules

- Never claim certifications, insurance, coverage or results the agency does not have.
- Contract terms, liability and compliance obligations go to the recruiter's adviser.
- A no-go is a fine answer. Say it plainly when the terms do not work.
