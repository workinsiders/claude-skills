# Client win/loss review

"They went another way" teaches nothing. You help a recruiter find out why a client was won or lost, so the next pitch and the next account plan improve. For a single lost search, use lost-search-post-mortem (Agency Desk Pack) instead; this is about the relationship or the pitch.

## Input

Whatever exists: the account history, placements, proposals and terms sent, emails, notes from calls, anything the client said about the decision.

## Step 1: timeline

Dated steps from first contact to the outcome: first call, proposal, terms, first job order, placements, problems, the decision. Mark gaps over three weeks.

## Step 2: expected versus actual

For each stage, what the recruiter expected to happen and what did. Where the two split is usually where the outcome was decided.

## Step 3: the reason

- **What the client said,** in their words, if anything.
- **What the evidence suggests,** marked as a guess to confirm.
- Check the usual causes: price, speed, candidate quality, communication, a relationship that was single-threaded, a new decision maker, an internal TA team, a competitor's relationship.

## Step 4: keep and change

- Two things to keep (for wins especially).
- Two to three changes, each tied to a skill or habit: "build a key client plan for any client over [n] placements a year", "ask for exclusivity on hard roles", "reconnect with the new HR leader within 30 days".

## Step 5: ask the client

Three short questions to send or ask, framed to learn, not to argue: what decided it, what we could have done differently, whether the door is open.

## Output

```
WIN/LOSS: [Client] | [won / lost / pitch lost] | [date]
Timeline: [ ]
Expected vs actual: [stage: expected / actual]
Reason: client said [ ] | evidence suggests [ ] (to confirm)
Keep: [ ] | Change: [ ]
Questions for the client: [ ]
```

## Rules

- Write about the process and the relationship, not people's character.
- Never guess the client's internal politics as fact. Mark it unknown.
- No blame. The point is the next account.
