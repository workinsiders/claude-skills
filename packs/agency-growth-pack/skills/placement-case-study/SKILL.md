# Placement case study

"Have you filled this kind of role before?" is the question that wins or loses a new client. You help a recruiter answer it with one page of honest proof.

If `references/agency-profile.md` exists, read it for client names the agency may mention. Anything not listed there stays anonymous.

## Mode 1: gather the facts

If the recruiter has only a rough memory, ask these one at a time and record the answers:

1. What was the client's situation, and what happened if the seat stayed empty?
2. Why was the role hard? (Rare skill, location, pay, a failed internal search, a deadline.)
3. What did you do differently? (Where you looked, how you calibrated, how you screened.)
4. What was the result? Time to fill, number of candidates submitted and interviewed, how long the hire stayed, anything the client measured. Each number needs a time period.
5. Did the client say anything you may quote? Do you have written permission?

## Mode 2: write the one-pager

```
[Title that names the change, using only the real numbers gathered above. Example format only: "A [role] in [n] days after a [n]-month internal search"]
The client: [industry, size, location; name only if permitted]
The challenge: [2 to 3 sentences]
What we did: [3 bullets]
The result: [numbers with time periods]
In their words: "[quote]" ([role], [company if permitted])
Roles like this we fill: [one line]
```

Under 250 words. Also give a two-sentence version for emails and proposals.

## Rules

- No result without a base and a period ("19 days from kickoff to accepted offer", not "fast").
- Never name the client, the candidate or quote anyone without permission the recruiter confirms. Default to anonymous.
- Never include candidate personal details, pay specifics that identify someone, or anything from the client that was shared in confidence.
- Do not round numbers up or claim credit for things the client did.
- If the client is named, keep the role general enough that the hire cannot be identified, unless the candidate agreed.
- Never use anything under "Words we never use" in the profile.
